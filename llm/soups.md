**1. Japanese Miso Soup**  
A silky, umami-packed Japanese staple that’s quick to make and often enjoyed as a breakfast side or light lunch.  
- 4 cups dashi stock (or water with kombu and bonito flakes)  
- 2–3 tablespoons white miso paste  
- ½ block firm tofu, cubed  
- 1–2 sheets nori (seaweed), torn into pieces  
- 1 green onion, thinly sliced  
- Optional: ½ cup sliced shiitake mushrooms  

**How to make it:**  
1. Gently heat the dashi in a pot over medium heat; do not let it boil once the miso will be added.  
2. In a small ladle, scoop a bit of hot dashi and whisk in the miso paste until smooth, then stir the mixture back into the pot.  
3. Add tofu and simmer gently for 2–3 minutes until heated through.  
4. Stir in torn nori and sliced green onion just before turning off the heat.  
5. Serve immediately in small bowls, garnishing with a bit of the floating nori and green onion.

**2. Thai Tom Yum Goong**  
A bold, aromatic hot-and-sour shrimp soup that balances spicy chilies, tangy lime, fragrant lemongrass, and galangal for a vibrant, refreshing bowl.  
- 1 liter chicken or vegetable stock  
- 2–3 stalks lemongrass, bruised and sliced thinly  
- 1-inch galangal, sliced into coins  
- 3–4 kaffir lime leaves, torn  
- 1–2 Thai bird’s eye chilies, sliced (adjust for heat)  
- 300g large shrimp, peeled and deveined  
- 1 tablespoon lime juice  
- 1 tablespoon fish sauce  
- 1 tablespoon chopped fresh cilantro  
- Optional: ½ cup straw or oyster mushrooms  

**How to make it:**  
1. In a pot, bring stock to a gentle simmer; add lemongrass, galangal, and lime leaves and let infuse for 5 minutes.  
2. Add chilies and mushrooms, cooking for another 3 minutes until fragrant.  
3. Add shrimp and cook just until they turn pink and opaque, about 2–3 minutes—don’t overcook!  
4. Stir in lime juice and fish sauce, adjusting seasoning to taste (more lime for acidity, more fish sauce for umami).  
5. Garnish with fresh cilantro and serve hot with jasmine rice or on its own.

**3. Vietnamese Pho Bo**  
A deeply aromatic, clear beef broth soup simmered with charred aromatics and spices, served over soft rice noodles and fresh herbs for a comforting, complex flavor profile.  
- 2 lbs beef bones (marrow and knuckle) or 1 lb bones + 1 lb beef brisket  
- 1 large onion, halved and charred over flame  
- 1 large ginger, halved and charred  
- 4–5 star anise  
- 1–2 cinnamon sticks  
- 1 teaspoon coriander seeds, lightly toasted  
- 1 teaspoon black peppercorns  
- 3–4 cups water (for initial blanch)  
- Fish sauce, to taste  
- Rice noodles (banh pho), cooked according to package  
- Fresh garnishes: Thai basil, bean sprouts, lime wedges, chili slices, cilantro  

**How to make it:**  
1. Blanch the bones in boiling water for 5 minutes, then drain and rinse to remove impurities.  
2. In a large pot, combine bones, charred onion, charred ginger, star anise, cinnamon, coriander seeds, and peppercorns; cover with fresh water and bring to a boil.  
3. Once boiling, reduce to a very gentle simmer, partially cover, and cook for 2–3 hours, skimming any foam that rises to the surface.  
4. After simmering, strain the broth, discard aromatics, and season with fish sauce to taste; keep warm.  
5. To serve, divide cooked rice noodles into bowls, ladle hot broth over top, and arrange fresh garnishes on the side for each person to customize.
